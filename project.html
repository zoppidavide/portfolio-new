Rebuilding Satispay app information architecture

Launched in 2015, Satispay set out to disrupt the European fintech landscape by introducing an independent payment network. By building a proprietary infrastructure, it successfully bypassed traditional banks and issuing circuits like Visa and Mastercard, significantly reducing transaction fees for merchants and enabling seamless micro-payments for everyday users.

By 2025, Satispay had grown into a big player, with over 5 million users and a network of 350,000+ merchants.To sustain this rapid growth and unlock new revenue streams, Satispay evolved from a payment tool into a multi-service financial app, expanding into meal vouchers, personal savings, PA payments, and more services.

The problem

As new business lines were added year after year, the app's core architecture faced a critical threshold. The primary design challenge was to integrate new feature without adding complexity to the experience, preserving the speed and simplicity of daily payment flows while actively driving engagement.

The second objective was to standardise the payment experience. By designing a universal entry point, we simplified the mental model for users, creating a seamless and predictable payment flow that handled both in store payments and peer-to-peer (P2P) transactions effortlessly.

Research

Users were central to our research strategy from day one. In the initial phase, we involved users to redefine the app’s feature hierarchy and architecture. We then conducted usability testing to validate the new information architecture, track Time-on-Task, and benchmark alternative navigation flows.

To ensure high-confidence data, we segmented testing across two distinct user cohorts:

  • Existing users: Evaluated how the redesign impacted established mental models, feature discovery, and daily friction.
  • New users: Tested the intuitiveness, learnability, and immediate value proposition of the new Home concept without prior product bias.

This dual-cohort approach delivered a rich balance of qualitative feedback and behavioral metrics, ensuring the redesigned Home felt intuitive for first-time users while remaining seamless for power users.

Solutions

The proposed solution features 5 tabs: Home, Accounts, Savings, Investments and Services. Along this primary navigation a “Pay Button” entry point to all the main payments flows.

The Home page is meant to be a place where user can check their balances to start a new operation as quickly as possible. While Accounts tab is the place where all the balances and other wallets of the user can be managed simply, giving the user more space.

All the other tabs are then developed on a vertical theme, so that the user can save, invest or discover services.

Impact

The redesign successfully validated the new Home experience with zero negative impact on primary metrics or core user segments. For new users, the updated flow directly boosted activation and revenue metrics, driving higher overall transaction volume, increased spend, and growth in generated fees.

Additionally, analyzing downstream user behavior revealed valuable engagement shifts across secondary features. For instance, taps on the Savings tab increased as users actively navigated to check their total balance, turning an architectural shift into a catalyst for organic feature discovery without increasing user friction.

Reflections and key learnings

Isolating key high-intent data points, metrics users actively check—created a strategic opportunity to surface relevant product features without disrupting the core user flow.

By placing high-value offerings alongside high-visibility touchpoints, we seamlessly exposed users to context-aware features, such as setting up an Invested Money Box. This strategy turned routine balance checks into moments of organic product discovery, directly supporting our monetization and cross-selling goals.
