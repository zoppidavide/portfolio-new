01 · Brief

Why paid plans, why now

The problem

Since its launch in 2015, Satispay's revenue has been tied to transactions, so it rises and falls with the seasons and with how much people spend each month. As the app grew into a full financial companion, with cards, IBAN, savings and investments, the company needed a steadier, recurring revenue stream. It also needed a broader offer to compete with Revolut and N26, which already sold tiered premium plans.

The solution

A subscription program, built in two steps. Plus launched in December 2025 at €3.99/month, built around everyday savings. In July 2026 it became a three-tier offer with Metal (€12.99/month) and Velvet (€45.99/month), each with its own Mastercard card, airport lounge access and partner perks.

As the product designer of the Growth domain, I designed every experience in the plans: how users discover them, choose and subscribe, manage, switch and cancel, and how they use their perks, from airport lounges and Fast Track to partner subscriptions.

The constraints

  • Choice: the top tier bundled up to 15 benefits. The paywall had to explain them without overloading people.
  • Discovery: users had to understand what a plan gives them in a few seconds, at the right moment, without the app feeling like an ad.
  • Lifecycle: upgrades, downgrades and cancellations each follow their own rules: a 14-day withdrawal period, pro-rata refunds, and card and lounge costs if you leave within 10 months. All of it had to be easy to read and compliant with EU consumer law.

02 · Research

What would people pay for?

We were asking people to pay for something they'd always had for free. Before designing the tiers, I mapped the subscription journeys of Revolut, N26 and Monzo, plus subscription-led products like Duolingo, Gemini and Preply. Using familiar patterns lowered the risk of a first-ever paid offer.

Then we ran rounds of tests with users to find out which benefits they valued most. In a survey with over 700 users, people repeatedly picked the most and least important benefit from small sets drawn from 11 categories. Try it yourself:

Video streaming and cinema carried over half of all preferences, with news in second place. VPNs sat near the bottom. Most people cared about three to five types of service, so we focused each plan on a few strong benefits instead of the longest possible list.

03 · A/B tests

Layout beat messaging

The first test pitted a paywall that led with how much you'd save against one that listed features. The difference wasn't significant, and that told us the layout mattered more than the message. So the second test changed the structure: a two-step paywall inspired by Revolut, where you pick a plan first and billing second.

04 · Key experiences

Where the design lives

Four surfaces carried most of the work: discovery, the paywall, plan management and the perks themselves.

Discovery through balanced upsells

Discovery relies mostly on upsells and banners placed where a benefit makes sense, so users get the picture quickly. Each touchpoint shows one benefit, at the moment users feel the cost of not having it. A frequency cap keeps them from feeling pushy.

A paywall built to compare

    Perks you use inside the app

    Metal and Velvet include access to 1,000+ airport lounges through DragonPass, plus Fast Track at security. The whole experience happens in Satispay: search an airport, browse lounges, buy or claim a pass and show a QR code at the entrance. No emails and no third-party app. Partner subscriptions like Corriere della Sera, La Gazzetta dello Sport and NordVPN are activated from the plan page in a few taps.

    06 · Learnings

    What the data taught me

    New users who choose a plan about 3 in 10
    01

    Plans are now part of how people use Satispay

    Plans started as an add-on and are now part of the Satispay ecosystem: the card, savings, investments and payments all connect to them. The clearest sign is that close to 30% of new users choose a plan when they start using the app.

    02

    Retention matters more than the benefit list

    Most partner perks were used by fewer than 10% of subscribers. The big exception was NordVPN, at around 40%, even though it ranked near the bottom in the survey. A long list helps someone subscribe, but they stay for the few benefits they use every week.

    03

    Some churn isn't a decision

    A large share of early cancellations came from users who didn't have enough balance on renewal day. They hadn't chosen to leave. Letting the renewal pull the missing amount from their bank account fixed this at the system level, which no cancellation screen could.

    04

    Be clear about the hard parts

    Withdrawal periods, pro-rata refunds and card costs are where a subscription loses or earns trust. Spelling out the numbers on every downgrade and cancellation, even when it added a step, was always worth it.

    07 · What's next

    Where plans go from here

    The program is live and generating signal every day. The direction for the next releases: meet people earlier, point them to the perks they'll actually use, and bring plans to more places.
